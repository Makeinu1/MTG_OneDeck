# O4P-01H-R: Object Taxonomy Matrix (draft)

Status: `analyzed-not-integrated`

Role: Requirements Analyst

Source boundary: this analysis uses only the pinned local Comprehensive Rules, effective June 19, 2026, at `rule/Magic_The_Gathering_Comprehensive_Rules.txt`. It does not choose identity strings, TypeScript names, an Online protocol, UI behavior, or a copiable-values algorithm.

## CR-grounded interpretation

CR 109.1 names seven direct object categories: an ability on the stack, a card, a copy of a card, a token, a spell, a permanent, and an emblem. The matrix makes three distinctions that are required by the other cited rules: a copy of a spell is a spell, a copy of an ability is an ability, and a source is a role that an object can have rather than an additional object identity. This yields the ten required analysis rows below without silently treating effects, statuses, or players as objects.

The matrix columns are: **object kind**, **CR definition and scope**, **valid locus and lifecycle**, **characteristics and status**, **owner and controller**, **stack relation**, **zone-change identity**, **copy and state-based-action rule**, **MVP classification**, and **explicit DEFER boundary**. Every row is populated in every column.

## Required cross-object invariants

1. **Object versus characteristic:** CR 109.3 limits characteristics to the listed fields. Controller, owner, target, attachment, tapped state, counters, and similar facts must not be silently treated as characteristics. CR 110.5a separately states that status is not a characteristic.
2. **Card versus token:** CR 108.2b and 111.6 make the distinction categorical. A card-sized representation does not make a token a card.
3. **Permanent boundary:** CR 110.1 makes battlefield membership the boundary for the permanent category. A permanent spell is still a spell until resolution.
4. **Stack boundary:** CR 405.1-4 requires card-backed spells and cardless abilities to be ordered as stack objects. CR 405.6 requires effects, static effects, special actions, turn-based actions, and state-based actions not to be modeled as stack objects merely because they change the game.
5. **Activation versus trigger:** CR 602.2a creates the activated-ability stack object during activation. CR 603.2-3 lets a triggered ability trigger first and places it on the stack at the next priority opportunity. These are distinct entry paths even though both produce an ability object.
6. **New-object default:** CR 400.7 is the default for zone changes. The 400.7a-m exceptions are narrow continuation permissions, not permission to retain object identity globally.
7. **Trigger timing around zone change:** CR 603.6-6c and 603.10 require destination-object lookup or look-back handling for the relevant zone-change triggers. CR 111.7-8 and 704.5d require token cleanup after applicable triggers can trigger.
8. **Copy cleanup:** CR 704.5e and 707.10a remove copies of spells outside the stack and copies of cards outside the stack or battlefield as state-based actions. This cleanup is distinct from countering, resolution, or ordinary zone movement.
9. **Copy scope:** CR 707.2 says copy effects take copiable values and specified stack decisions, while status, counters, stickers, and other noncopiable effects are not copied. This draft records the boundary only; it does not specify the algorithm.
10. **Source continuity is relational:** CR 113.7a and 707.10b preserve source lookup semantics for an ability or its copy without making the source a second copy of the ability or preventing the source from becoming a new object after a zone change.

## MVP classification summary

The MVP object surface is: card, permanent, token, spell, ability on the stack, emblem, uncast card copy, copy spell, and copy ability, plus source lookup as a derived relation. MVP means the rules distinction and lifecycle boundary must be represented in the later contract; it does not authorize an implementation in this lane. The minimum observable behavior is deterministic classification, stack-versus-zone placement, owner/controller distinction, new-object-on-zone-change default, and the two state-based-action cleanup boundaries in 704.5d-e.

The following are explicitly outside MVP for this lane: full effect execution; casting, activation, trigger-placement, priority, APNAP, and resolution engines; continuous/replacement/prevention effects; last-known-information storage; attachments, counters, stickers, and status transition algorithms; complete command-zone and multiplayer rules; hidden-zone secrecy; card/Oracle data; and all copiable-values or copy-dependency algorithms.
