# Boundary Lifecycle Contract

<!-- clause: BOUNDARY-001 -->
An operation is Boundary-applicable when its semantic correctness can remain unresolved across a meaningful lifecycle boundary, including when any of these is true: (1) semantic work can remain pending after its initiating owned synchronous transition returns; (2) owner, participant, referenced identity, topology, parent operation, session, or authority lifetime can change before terminalization; (3) the operation can cross an async, subsystem, process, persistence, reconnect/retry, or session/generation boundary; or (4) another actor, component, or subsystem can observe or act on intermediate or result state before participating authorities have converged. Pure, local, synchronous helpers remain outside the registry. An asynchronous read-only operation with no retained semantic state or authority is not automatically applicable.

<!-- clause: BOUNDARY-002 -->
Every registered operation is described across exactly seven axes: Authority, Identity, Commit, Invalidation, Persistence, Publication, and Receipt/Reconciliation. The domain contract that owns an axis remains the authority for that axis's behavior.

<!-- clause: BOUNDARY-003 -->
Each axis is one of `COVERED`, `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `MISSING`, or `OWNER_DECISION_REQUIRED`. `Authority`, `Identity`, and `Commit` are core axes and cannot be `NOT_APPLICABLE`; when their semantic answer is absent, they are `MISSING` unless a canonical, currently-unresolved M1 owner decision actually governs that axis answer, in which case they are `OWNER_DECISION_REQUIRED`. Only `Invalidation`, `Persistence`, `Publication`, and `Receipt/Reconciliation` may be `NOT_APPLICABLE`, and each requires its own bounded reason: `Invalidation` uses `NO_INVALIDATION_SOURCE`, `Persistence` uses `NO_PERSISTENCE_REQUIRED`, `Publication` uses `NO_PUBLICATION_REQUIRED`, and `Receipt/Reconciliation` uses `NO_RECEIPT_PATH`. These reasons are not interchangeable. `NOT_APPLICABLE` does not mean unimplemented, not investigated, unknown, deferred, or lacking a current test; those conditions are `MISSING` unless a currently-unresolved canonical M1 owner decision actually governs the missing axis answer. `OWNER_DECISION_REQUIRED` references only canonical IDs of currently-unresolved M1 owner decisions that govern the missing axis answer; it is not free-form prose and does not itself supply the semantic answer. Once such a decision is resolved, `OWNER_DECISION_REQUIRED` is no longer valid for that axis, and the row must be updated to `COVERED`, `MISSING`, or a valid `NOT_APPLICABLE` state as appropriate. `COVERED` is a reviewed M2 assertion that the referenced active semantic clauses, read in the scope of this specific operation, collectively define the relevant axis. Mere existence of an active clause is insufficient; implementation behavior or tests alone are insufficient; and a conceptually related clause is insufficient if it does not define that operation axis. The machine checker validates structural and referential integrity only and cannot prove natural-language semantic relevance. `MISSING` contains no answer.

<!-- clause: BOUNDARY-004 -->
Operation completeness is derived from its seven axis states and is not independently authorable. `OWNER_DECISION_REQUIRED` wins first, then any `MISSING` yields `INCOMPLETE`, otherwise the result is `COMPLETE`. A coverage result remains distinct from an implementation-defect verdict.

<!-- clause: BOUNDARY-005 -->
This contract preserves M0 Product Truth, M1 NOW, M2 semantic meaning, the semantic map as a coverage index only, M3 evidence, M4 bounded work, and M6 execution continuation. It does not invent domain semantics, replace an owning contract, or turn a coverage row into delivery state. A row is an explicit completeness assertion for one operation; absence of a row makes no completeness assertion and does not imply `COMPLETE` or exhaustive registration.
